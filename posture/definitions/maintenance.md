# Maintenance

## Stance

Existing public behavior and established structure are presumptively intentional. Prefer preserving known contracts while making the smallest coherent change.

## Defaults

- Prefer localized changes.
- Compatibility is presumptively valuable.
- Preserve established behavior unless evidence or an explicit requirement justifies changing it.
- Expand scope conservatively, but follow directly coupled consequences when the requested correction cannot otherwise be completed coherently.
- Removal requires stronger evidence than preservation.

## Boundary

These are decision priors, not conclusions. Do not preserve a structure merely because it exists when direct evidence shows that it is erroneous, unsafe, obsolete, or explicitly superseded.

Do not let preservation create fusion when replacement is clearly required.
